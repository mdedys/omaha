# Protection is ownership, with one hold time

Who blocks whom is decided once at the snap by ownership, never by a roll or a rating. On a slide, each lineman owns a gap (full-line slide) and the RB owns the far edge. On man, each lineman owns the DL in front of him (big on big) and the backs take the leftover rushers inside-out. A rusher nobody owns, or whose owner already has a rusher, is free. Every blocked rusher is held until one global hold time, twice that when double-teamed, so every sack traces to a named free rusher or to the QB holding the ball too long.

## Considered Options

- **Pure count.** Each blocker takes the nearest rusher and a rusher is free only when rushers outnumber blockers. The line call would barely matter, and matching the slide to the look is the protection puzzle.
- **Authored matchups.** The puzzle says who blocks whom for each of the nine protections, which is too slow to author.
- **Per-matchup timers or ratings.** A block that holds longer for one lineman than another is a hidden rating the player can't see.
- **A team-level sack roll**, as in Football GM. Not deterministic, and it names no rusher.
- **Gap ownership for man as well.** One mechanism for every call, but a lineman would let his DL go when a second rusher hit the same gap, which a real man call never does.

## Consequences

- Half-slide protections aren't expressible: "slide left" is always a full-line slide.
- Only the DL role counts as a lineman's man, so a stand-up edge rusher with the LB role is a back's or nobody's on a man call.
